# Pangwali Language Archive

## Language Documentation, Preservation and Digital Resources

The Pangwali Language Archive is a community-oriented language documentation project focused on Pangwali, a low-resource language spoken primarily in the Pangi Valley of Chamba district, Himachal Pradesh, India.

The project aims to document spoken Pangwali, preserve linguistic knowledge, and develop structured language resources that can support linguistic research and future language-technology applications.

## Objectives

* Document spoken Pangwali through field recordings.
* Collect speech from speakers across different villages and regions of Pangi.
* Document vocabulary, sentences, conversations, stories and other forms of natural speech.
* Record and investigate variation between local speech communities.
* Build structured audio and text datasets.
* Preserve linguistic information together with relevant speaker, location and recording metadata.
* Develop resources that can support future NLP, speech recognition, translation and other language-technology research.

## Pangwali and Linguistic Variation

Pangwali is spoken across the Pangi Valley and shows geographical and community-level variation.

Existing linguistic sources describe four major regional varieties:

* Killar
* Purthi
* Sach
* Dharwasi

Fieldwork may identify additional local terms for speech varieties. For example, speakers have reported the names Bhyunshi and Banoshi. These are currently recorded as speaker-reported varieties and are not assumed to represent formally established dialect classifications.

The project therefore aims to document both established linguistic classifications and terminology used by speakers themselves.

## pgg/app

pgg/app is the fieldwork application being developed to support the collection and management of language data.

The application is designed for fieldwork environments where internet connectivity may be limited. The primary design principle is offline-first data collection, allowing recordings and metadata to be stored locally and synchronized with cloud storage when an internet connection becomes available.

Planned capabilities include:

* Audio recording
* Speaker management
* Language and speech-variety management
* Recording metadata
* Consent management
* Local data storage
* Offline data collection
* Cloud synchronization
* Download and restore of field data
* Dataset export

The application is currently maintained in a separate branch:

[pgg/app Field Work App](https://github.com/karu03/pangwali-language-archive/tree/Pangwali-Field-Work-App)

## Linguistic Research

An important component of the project is documenting linguistic variation across Pangi.

The project aims to investigate differences in:

* Vocabulary
* Pronunciation
* Grammar
* Expressions
* Speech patterns
* Local terminology
* Mutual intelligibility between communities

Speaker-reported information will be retained separately from externally established linguistic classifications where the classification has not yet been verified.

## Language Technology

The resulting resources may support future research and development in:

* Automatic speech recognition
* Speech-to-text
* Text-to-speech
* Machine translation
* Natural language processing
* Low-resource language modelling
* OCR and document processing
* Pangwali-Hindi language resources
* Devanagari and Takri-related language resources

The project prioritizes the collection of reliable and well-documented data before large-scale model development.

## Data and Research Ethics

The project involves collecting information and recordings from individuals and communities. The project aims to follow responsible documentation practices, including:

* Obtaining informed consent from speakers.
* Respecting speaker preferences regarding attribution and use of recordings.
* Maintaining accurate metadata.
* Preserving original speaker terminology.
* Distinguishing community reports from verified linguistic classifications.
* Handling sensitive cultural or personal information responsibly.
* Using collected resources for appropriate research, documentation and language-technology purposes.
